# Emission podcast: script review notes

Source: lecture-prep script based on Chapter 3, "Emission," of Sönke Johnsen, *The Optics of Life* (Princeton, 2012).

## Corrections made to the script before recording

1. **Mammal thermal peak.** Original said ~12 µm. Wien's law gives 2898 µm·K / 310 K ≈ 9.3 µm for a per-wavelength spectrum. Changed to "a little under ten micrometers."
2. **Leaf infrared reflectance.** Original said high infrared reflectance "might also affect radiative heat loss," attributed to Johnsen. Leaves reflect strongly in the *near* infrared (~0.7–1.3 µm), but a 300 K leaf radiates at ~8–14 µm, where leaf emissivity is ~0.95–0.98. High NIR reflectance therefore bears on solar heat gain, not on radiative heat loss. Rewritten with the correct physics and the Johnsen attribution removed.
3. **Elephant hawkmoth.** Attribution changed from "Johnsen mentions" to Almut Kelber's group (Kelber, Balkenius & Warrant 2002, *Nature*), since I could not confirm the example appears in Chapter 3 specifically. The claim itself (color discrimination at dim starlight levels) is correct.
4. **Ozone and UV.** Sharpened "removes much of the ultraviolet" to "essentially all UVC and much of the UVB," which is the actual atmospheric behavior.

## Additions (all standard physics, added for clarity)

- Fourth-power example: doubling absolute temperature gives 16× the emitted power.
- Solar peak near 500 nm (per wavelength) at ~5800 K.
- Angular diameter of the sun, about 0.5°.
- Twilight elevation bands: civil 0–6°, nautical 6–12°, astronomical 12–18°.
- Named the ozone Chappuis band (broad, centered ~600 nm) as the twilight absorption feature.
- Full moon ≈ 10× brighter than a quarter moon (opposition effect); full moons ride high in winter, low in summer.
- Moonlight is slightly redder than sunlight because lunar regolith is reddish.
- Upwelling light in clear ocean is ~1–2% of downwelling, which is why a white belly fails.
- Wintergreen candy mechanism: sugar-crystal fracture discharges in the UV, methyl salicylate fluoresces blue.
- Light-pollution section notes the book predates the LED transition.

## Verified as stated (no change)

Stefan–Boltzmann T⁴ scaling in energy units; Kirchhoff's law (absorptivity = emissivity per wavelength at equilibrium); Planck curves never cross (hotter emits more at every wavelength); peak location depends on per-wavelength vs per-frequency representation; color temperature definition and the blue-sky caveat; sun moves ~1° per 4 minutes; airglow oxygen line at 557.7 nm; zodiacal light and integrated starlight as moonless-night sources; lunar cues for coral spawning; bioluminescence as enzyme-mediated chemiluminescence via luciferin/luciferase, photocytes, bacterial symbionts, secretions; marine dominance and freshwater rarity of bioluminescence; function list; counterillumination with spectral tuning in some squid; blue-green peak (~450–550 nm, often ~480 nm) and rarity of red; red emission produced by wavelength conversion in some fishes (e.g., *Malacosteus*); hydrothermal-vent light exceeding blackbody prediction at shorter wavelengths (White et al. 2002); snapping-shrimp sonoluminescence (Lohse et al. 2001); mantis-shrimp cavitation without documented sonoluminescence (Patek & Caldwell 2005).
